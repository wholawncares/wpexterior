Roof replacement cost in Des Plaines: what to expect

Guides publish replacement ranges that conflict by thousands. This post shows them side by side, with sources and dates — and explains why no guide figure is a price.

W
WP Exterior IncNovember 2026 · 9 min read

Every figure in this guide is a third-party guide or competitor range, not this business’s price. The guides conflict by thousands of dollars because they measure different things — different roof sizes, different inclusions, different methods — and they are shown conflicting on purpose. Pricing for a particular roof comes from a written on-site estimate.

One square equals 100 square feet of roof surface, not living area. That distinction matters: a 2,000-square-foot house can have a 2,500-square-foot roof once pitch and overhangs are counted.

What a roof replacement costs in Des Plaines in 2026: the guide ranges

No Des Plaines-specific pricing exists except one undated competitor page ($10,000–$25,000 “typically”, marketing content, identity unverified). The ranges below are Chicago and Illinois guides, shown with sources and dates:

  • Angi, Chicago, updated 9 July 2026: most projects $6,049–$14,515, average $10,185.
  • Modernize, Illinois, updated 27 August 2026: $9,500–$23,000, average $15,200.
  • XL, Illinois, 2026: $8,500–$19,000, about $13,000 average.
  • ASI, 2026: $9,000–$40,000 overall; most single-family architectural $13,500–$22,000.
  • SmartRoofingCalculator, November 2026, 25-square architectural including tear-off: Chicago $15,045–$21,830; Illinois $12,750–$18,500.
  • MyRoofData, Chicago, 2026: $15,000–$26,000.
  • Green Attic, January 2026: $13,800–$22,500.

The Angi average sits well below the modelled totals because the methods differ: customer surveys across all job sizes, including partial jobs, against modelled full tear-off on a defined roof. Neither figure is “wrong”; neither is the Des Plaines price. The gap is a reason to read ranges as context and get a written estimate — not to pick the lowest number.

  • Green Attic: from $550 per square, architectural.
  • MyRoofData, before tear-off: 3-tab $408–$642, architectural $525–$875; tear-off $100–$200 per square per layer.

Per-square-foot figures conflict in the same way: architectural asphalt $5.00–$7.50 (XL), $5–$11 (Modernize), $4–$6 (Holda), $8–$12 (Chicago Roofing Team). The spread is the point: per-unit figures from different guides, with different inclusions, cannot be compared without knowing what is included.

What affects roof replacement cost in Des Plaines IL

Six factors move a replacement price more than any other, and each is assessed on site:

Roof size and pitch

Area drives material and labor; pitch drives access difficulty and safety requirements. Steep slopes take longer and cost more per square than walkable ones.

Layers and tear-off

Des Plaines code requires tear-off when two or more layers already exist, when the base is water-soaked or deteriorated, or when the base is shake, slate, or tile; at most two layers may exist on completion. Each tear-off layer adds disposal cost, and the guides price tear-off at $100–$200 per square per layer (MyRoofData).

Decking condition

Rotten or water-damaged decking must be replaced, and its extent is only known once the old covering is off. Written scopes should state how decking replacement is priced.

Chimneys, skylights, and penetrations

Each penetration needs flashing and detailing. Chimneys, skylights, vents, and pipes add labor and materials, and chimney flashing is a repeat leak point. See the skylight installation page for skylight topics.

Ventilation and insulation

Ridge vents, soffit vents, and attic insulation are often addressed during replacement. The Des Plaines guide’s ventilation shorthand (1:150, or 1:300 with roof and soffit vents) omits the IRC’s conditions and must not be treated as the full rule. Ventilation topics are on the roof ventilation installation page.

Gutters and edge details

Drip edge, fascia, and gutter work are frequently part of a replacement scope. Gutters are on the gutter installation page. Edge detailing affects both cost and leak resistance.

How long does a roof replacement take in Des Plaines Illinois?

No duration is promised here, and no guide figure settles it: duration depends on roof size, pitch, layers, weather, decking condition, and crew size. What can be said is what affects duration — the same factors listed above, plus weather interruptions in Des Plaines’ freeze–thaw and storm seasons.

Winter-work feasibility is a further question: sources differ on whether winter replacement is practical, and this site makes no winter installation promise. Ask what the business’s position is, and get scheduling terms in writing. A scope that names start and completion expectations in general terms, with weather caveats, is more useful than a day count.

Is roof replacement covered by insurance in Des Plaines?

No coverage claim is made here. Whether replacement is covered depends on the policy, the cause of damage, and the policy’s terms — storm damage, wear, and maintenance are treated differently, and deductibles, limits, and exclusions all apply. No deadline or coverage rule is stated as fact in this guide.

The process in general terms: document the damage with dated photographs, arrange an inspection with a written report, and file the claim as the homeowner. A contractor must not represent the homeowner on a claim or file it on the homeowner’s behalf (secondary summaries of 815 ILCS 513/18(i)); an estimate, documentation, and conferring with the insurer’s representative are described as not barred. Negotiating or settling a claim requires a public-adjuster license.

What to watch out for when you get quotes

  • Quotes without a written scope. Illinois roofing contracts must include contact details, scope and dates, insurer identification where insurance is involved, a 72-hour rescission clause, a bold-faced trust-account statement, and a deductible non-waiver statement (225 ILCS 335 §5.5). A quote that is only a number is not a scope.
  • Comparing different inclusions. Tear-off, disposal, underlayment, ice barrier, flashing, ventilation, permits, and decking are included by some guides and excluded by others. Compare scopes line by line.
  • Deductible offers. A contractor offering to absorb or rebate a deductible is a red flag: secondary summaries describe a ban on such advertising or promises.
  • Unverified licensing. Verify the license on the IDFPR License Lookup. The city may not issue a roofing permit without proof of current licensure.
  • Pressure to sign. The 72-hour rescission clause exists for a reason. No legitimate scope needs a same-day signature.

When to call a professional

Call when the roof shows widespread wear, repeated leaks, or storm damage, or when a replacement decision needs inspection findings behind it. See the roof inspection page for what an inspection covers, and call during listed hours to discuss scope and terms.
