How much does a roof inspection cost in Des Plaines

Inspection cost guides disagree because they describe different kinds of inspection. Here is what the guides actually publish, and what to ask before booking.

W
WP Exterior IncNovember 2026 · 8 min read

There is no single published price for a roof inspection in Des Plaines, and the guides that do publish figures disagree — because they are describing different things: a quick visual check tied to a repair quote, a comprehensive on-site inspection with a written report, and an independent inspection for a sale or an insurance question. This guide shows the attributed ranges side by side, explains what changes the price, and states plainly what is not known.

Every figure below is a third-party guide range with its source and date. None of them is this business’s price. WP Exterior Inc’s inspection terms are not stated on this site — call (224) 802-4866 during listed hours to confirm them.

What guides publish about inspection cost

No Des Plaines-specific inspection pricing was found in the research behind this guide. The closest published figures are Chicago-area and national guides, and they conflict:

  • Holda, northwest suburbs guide: a comprehensive on-site inspection in the Chicago suburbs is “commonly ~$100–$400”, distinguished from a free preliminary photo or video estimate.
  • Chicago Storm Pros: paid independent inspections in Chicagoland “generally $200–$600”.
  • Second-hand national figures via RNS Roofing (updated 30 October 2026, citing Fixr and Inch Calculator): $307–$571 (Fixr); average ~$225, range $75–$850; walk-on $250–$500; drone $350–$650; infrared $500–$800. These are second-hand and should be checked against the original guides before being relied on.

The ranges overlap but do not agree, because the inspections differ in scope, reporting, and method. Do not average them, and do not treat any one of them as “the” Des Plaines price. What an inspection costs for a particular roof is settled by asking the business that will do it — including whether a written report is included and what it covers.

Why some inspections are advertised as free

Chicagoland sources describe two different models. Many contractors advertise free inspections tied to prospective work: the inspection is part of quoting a repair or replacement. Paid independent inspections are the norm where neutrality matters — for a home sale, or for an insurance question where the report must stand on its own.

“Free” in the first model is a pricing claim about a particular business’s terms, and it must not be assumed. This site makes no “free inspection” claim for WP Exterior Inc. Before booking, ask whether the inspection is charged, what triggers a charge, whether a written report is included, and whether the inspection obliges you to accept any recommended work.

A useful distinction from the guides: a free preliminary photo or video estimate is not the same as a comprehensive on-site inspection. Ask which one is being offered, and what each one includes.

What actually changes the price of an inspection

Guides point to the same set of drivers. Each one changes what the inspector must do, how long it takes, or what the report must contain:

Roof size and pitch

A larger roof has more surface, more flashing, and more penetrations to check. A steeper pitch changes access and the time needed to cover each slope safely. Both affect the scope of the visit, not just its price.

Roof material

Asphalt shingles, metal panels, wood shake, and flat membranes fail differently and are checked differently. A roof with mixed materials — for example, a pitched shingle roof with a flat porch section — needs each system looked at on its own terms.

Access and obstructions

Trees overhanging the roof, limited ladder placement, locked gates, and attic access all affect what can be inspected. An inspection that cannot reach the attic cannot report on attic conditions, ventilation, or deck underside signs — ask what is included when access is limited.

Report type

A verbal summary, a written findings report with photographs, and a formal report for a sale or an insurer are different products. The report type is often the largest single driver of price, and it is the part most worth confirming before booking.

How long does a roof inspection take in Des Plaines?

No duration is promised here, and guides do not publish a single reliable figure. In general terms, the time depends on roof size, pitch, material, access, and whether the attic is included — the same drivers that change the price. A small single-slope check takes less time than a full inspection with attic review and a written photographic report.

When booking, ask what the visit includes and how the findings will be delivered, rather than relying on a duration figure. The useful question is not how long the inspector is on site, but what is examined and what is written down.

Roof inspection for a home sale in Des Plaines

For a sale, the distinction between a contractor inspection and an independent inspection matters. A contractor inspection is tied to prospective work; an independent inspection is a neutral report on condition. Buyers, sellers, and lenders may each want a different kind of document, and an inspection does not by itself satisfy any insurer or lender requirement — ask the party requiring the report what form it must take.

On Des Plaines’ mostly mid-century stock (median year built 1966, ACS 2019–2023), a pre-sale inspection commonly looks at layers already on the roof, deck condition, flashing at chimneys and walls, and attic ventilation — all topics that affect what a buyer should expect. Structure age is not roof age: the report should describe the roof as found, not infer its age from the house.

What to watch out for when you book an inspection

  • Confirm the price model. Ask whether the inspection is charged, what is included, and whether a written report with photographs is part of it.
  • Confirm the scope. Surface, flashing, valleys, penetrations, ridge, gutters and fascia, and accessible attic conditions are the usual checklist in guides — ask which are covered.
  • Do not assume credentials. This site makes no claim about inspector certifications, drone capability, or infrared capability for any business. Ask what methods will be used.
  • Get findings in writing. A written report with photographs is more useful than a verbal summary, especially if the findings will be shared with a buyer or an insurer.
  • Check the license separately. Illinois roofing licenses can be checked on the IDFPR License Lookup; the hiring guide on this blog explains Limited vs Unlimited licenses.

When to call a professional

An inspection is worth arranging after hail or wind events, before winter, when buying or selling, and when a roof is showing age — curling shingles, granule loss, or recurring stains.
