How to Repair a Flat Roof Leak in Des Plaines, Illinois

Flat roof leaks usually start at seams, flashing, drains, or ponding low spots. This guide explains the causes and the decision points — not step-by-step repair instructions.

W
WP Exterior IncNovember 2026 · 9 min read

Flat and low-slope roofs leak differently from pitched roofs: water moves slowly, ponds in low spots, and finds seams, flashing joints, and drains. This guide explains why flat roofs leak, how a leak’s source is narrowed down, what repair decisions involve, and when to call a roofer. It deliberately does not give step-by-step patching instructions — membrane repair is professional scope, and the wrong patch on the wrong system makes things worse.

Flat and low-slope sections on Des Plaines homes are most often additions, porch roofs, dormers, and garage roofs, plus flat roofs on mixed-use and mid-rise buildings downtown. The tie-in where a low-slope section meets a pitched roof is a repeat leak point in contractor guidance.

Why Flat Roofs Leak in Des Plaines

The causes are a short list, and most leaks involve more than one:

  • Ponding. Water that stands on a low-slope roof stresses seams and flashing and finds the weakest joint. Drainage — slope, drains, and scuppers — is the underlying question.
  • Seams. Membrane seams are the joints of the system. Freeze–thaw cycling stresses adhesives and welds, and EPDM seam and adhesive vulnerability under temperature swings is noted in general guidance.
  • Flashing and penetrations. Curbs, vents, pipes, and wall junctions are where membranes terminate — and where movement opens gaps.
  • Tie-ins. The transition where a low-slope membrane meets a pitched covering needs detailing that handles two systems moving differently.
  • Age and UV. Membranes age, shrink, and crack; ponding and debris accelerate the process.
  • Foot traffic and punctures. Maintenance traffic, fallen branches, and rooftop equipment can puncture or abrade a membrane.

Pitch limits explain why membranes exist at all: the Des Plaines guide states tab shingles not below 2:12 and roll roofing not below 1:12, so low slopes need membrane systems. Which systems a particular business works with — TPO, EPDM, modified bitumen, PVC — is a question for the call; each is confirmed separately, and none is assumed here.

How Do I Find the Leak on a Flat Roof?

On a flat roof, the interior stain is rarely below the entry point: water travels along the deck and under the membrane before it shows. Narrowing the source means checking, in order: drains and scuppers for blockages, seams in the area uphill from the stain, flashing at curbs and walls, penetrations, and ponding low spots after rain.

From inside, note where water shows relative to drains, curbs, and walls, and photograph with dates. From the ground or a safe vantage point, look for visible ponding, displaced flashing, and debris blocking drainage. Walking a flat roof to search is professional scope — membranes puncture, wet surfaces are slippery, and the search itself can create new damage.

Professional leak-finding may use flood testing, electronic methods, or core sampling, depending on the system. Those methods are described here in general terms only; what methods a business uses is a question for the call.

What to Record Before Calling

Before calling, record: where water shows inside and when it first appeared; whether it follows rain, snowmelt, or both; the location of drains, curbs, and penetrations relative to the stain; any visible ponding after rain; and the roof’s history as far as it is known — prior repairs, coatings, and approximate age. Dated photographs of interior signs and visible exterior conditions are the most useful record.

No repair materials are listed here, deliberately. Membrane patches must match the existing system — the wrong adhesive, primer, or patch material on TPO, EPDM, or modified bitumen can fail or damage the membrane — and that matching is professional knowledge, not a homeowner purchase.

What a Flat-Roof Repair Involves

A flat-roof repair decision moves through stages, each of which is professional scope. They are described here so a homeowner can follow what a roofer proposes — not as instructions:

1. Finding sound material

The repair area must be assessed for how far damage extends — wet insulation, deteriorated decking, and failed seams beyond the visible leak. Repairing over wet or unsound material does not hold.

2. Matching the system

The membrane type determines the repair method and materials. TPO, EPDM, modified bitumen, and PVC each have their own patching approach, and mixing systems without compatibility knowledge causes failures.

3. Addressing drainage

If ponding caused the leak, the repair must address drainage — slope, drains, or scuppers — or the same seam will fail again. A patch without a drainage answer is temporary by nature.

4. Sealing and detailing

Seams, edges, and terminations are detailed to the system’s requirements, and flashing at curbs and walls is checked as part of the same repair. The repair’s scope should be written down before work starts.

Coatings: What Guides Say, and What They Do Not Settle

Coatings are discussed in guides as a way to extend a sound flat roof’s life, not as a fix for a failed one. A coating over wet insulation, failed seams, or a deteriorated deck does not solve the underlying problem, and coating is a standalone service only where a business confirms it — roof coating is not a service page on this site, and no coating claim is made here.

Whether a coating suits a particular roof depends on the membrane, its condition, drainage, and the coating’s compatibility — all assessed on site. Ask what assessment precedes any coating proposal, and get the scope in writing.

What to Watch Out For

  • Patching without finding the source. On flat roofs, the stain and the entry point are often far apart. A patch in the wrong place wastes the repair.
  • Ignoring drainage. Ponding is a cause, not just a symptom. Repairs that leave ponding in place invite repeat failure.
  • Mismatched materials. Patches and coatings must suit the existing membrane system. Ask how the system was identified.
  • Chicago code imports. Reflectance and insulation requirements cited for Chicago flat roofs do not automatically apply in Des Plaines. Code questions are for the Des Plaines Building Division.
  • Commercial assumptions. Flat-roof guides often describe commercial buildings. Residential low-slope sections — additions, porches, dormers — are a different scope; confirm which scope applies.

When to Call a Professional

Call a roofer when water is entering, when ponding persists after rain, when seams or flashing look displaced, or when a previous patch has failed. Can I patch a flat roof leak myself in Des Plaines IL?
