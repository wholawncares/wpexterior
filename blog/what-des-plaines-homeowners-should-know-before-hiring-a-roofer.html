What Des Plaines homeowners should know before hiring a roofer

Before hiring a roofer in Des Plaines, verify the license, read the contract, and ask the right questions. This guide explains the Illinois licensing system and the checks that matter.

W
WP Exterior IncNovember 2026 · 11 min read

Hiring a roofer is a licensing, contract, and scope decision before it is a price decision. This guide explains how Illinois roofing licensing works, how to verify a license, what a roofing contract must contain, what questions to ask, and the red flags that matter — all from the statute and city sources read for this site.

Nothing in this guide states this business’s license status. That status is for the business to confirm and for the reader to verify on the IDFPR License Lookup.

What should you know before hiring a roofer in Des Plaines?

Three things before anything else: Illinois licenses roofing contractors at the state level; the city may not issue a roofing permit without proof of current licensure; and the contract itself is regulated — its contents are prescribed by statute, not left to custom.

The framework is the Illinois Roofing Industry Licensing Act (225 ILCS 335), rewritten by Public Act 104-427 effective 15 August 2025, with a sunset of 1 January 2031, administered by the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (IDFPR). The Act covers residential and commercial roofing; it is the state license that matters, not a city license — Des Plaines issues permits, not roofer licenses.

The rest of this guide walks through verification, questions, contract contents, deposits, red flags, and cost — in that order, because verification comes before price.

How do you verify a roofer’s license and insurance in Des Plaines IL?

Verification is a lookup, not a conversation:

  1. Ask for the license number and the exact licensed name. Both must appear on all contracts, bids, advertising, and commercial vehicles; knowing failure to display them is a Class A misdemeanor with a $1,000 fine.
  2. Check the IDFPR License Lookup. Enter the number and confirm the license is current, the name matches, and the class covers the work. Limited covers residential property of eight units or fewer ($10,000 bond); Unlimited covers all property ($25,000 bond). A W-2 qualifying party and a state exam are required for licensure.
  3. Confirm the permit position. The city may not issue a roofing permit without proof of current licensure. If a contractor says no permit is needed, ask how that was determined — the 25%-in-12-months trigger in a city handout is unresolved against the IRC’s permit requirements, and no blanket “under 25% needs no permit” rule is stated here.
  4. Ask about insurance directly. Insurance minimums stated in secondary summaries are unverified and are not stated here. Ask what coverage the business carries and request documentation; do not accept a verbal assurance.

Insurance documentation is a question for the business. This site makes no claim about any business’s insurance status, and no reader should infer one from this guide.

What questions should you ask a roofer in Des Plaines?

The questions below are drawn from the statute, the city guide, and the research. Ask them all, and note the answers:

  • What is your Illinois roofing license number and exact licensed name? Then verify on the IDFPR License Lookup.
  • What is the written scope? Materials, layers, tear-off, underlayment, ice barrier, flashing, ventilation, and decking — each named, not implied.
  • Will a permit be pulled, and who pulls it? The final inspection is required by the guide. Ask who handles the permit and the inspection.
  • What are the scheduling terms? Start and completion expectations in general terms, with weather caveats. No timeline promise is made on this site.
  • What happens if decking is damaged? How is decking replacement priced, and how is the homeowner told before it is done?
  • What is the payment schedule? See the deposit section below. Get it in writing.
  • What documentation will I receive? Photographs, a written report, and a written scope — before work starts, not after.
  • What is the warranty position? Ask, and get the answer in writing. This site makes no warranty claim.

What a roofing contract in Des Plaines needs to spell out

Section 5.5 of the Roofing Industry Licensing Act prescribes contract contents. The statute text was read for this guide; the contract must include:

  • Contact details for the contractor and the homeowner.
  • Scope and dates — what will be done, and when.
  • Insurer identification where insurance is involved.
  • A 72-hour rescission clause.
  • A bold-faced trust-account statement.
  • A deductible non-waiver statement.

Cancellation-period formulations in secondary sources conflict, so no single cancellation period is stated here beyond the statutory 72-hour rescission clause. If a contract lacks any of the prescribed items, ask why before signing. 815 ILCS 513 was not read for this guide and is not cited here.

On insurance: secondary summaries describe a contractor as barred from representing the homeowner, filing a claim, or inspecting without express permission, with an estimate, documentation, and conferring with the insurer’s representative described as not barred. Negotiating or settling a claim requires a public-adjuster license. No claim deadline or coverage rule is stated as fact.

How much deposit should you pay a roofer in Des Plaines Illinois?

No deposit figure is published here, because no deposit figure appears in the sources relied on for this guide. That is the honest answer: any deposit percentage quoted without a source would be invented.

What can be said in general terms: get the payment schedule in writing before work starts, including what is due at signing, at material delivery, and at completion; be wary of large upfront payments as general guidance; and remember the 72-hour rescission clause. Ask the business directly what its payment terms are, and compare the written terms across contractors rather than comparing verbal assurances.

Red flags when hiring a roofer in Des Plaines

  • No license number, or a number that does not verify. The license number and exact licensed name must appear on contracts, bids, and advertising.
  • Deductible offers. Advertising or promising to pay or rebate any part of a deductible is described in secondary summaries as banned. A contractor offering to absorb a deductible is a red flag.
  • Pressure to sign immediately. The 72-hour rescission clause exists for a reason. No scope needs a same-day signature.
  • No written scope. A number without materials, layers, flashing, ventilation, and decking terms is not a scope.
  • Out-of-area crews after storms. Verify licensing, get a written scope, and check for a real local address. Storm-chasing crews are a recurring pattern in contractor guidance.
  • Cash-only or no-permit proposals. The city may not issue a roofing permit without proof of current licensure. A proposal that avoids the permit process avoids the inspection that protects the homeowner.
